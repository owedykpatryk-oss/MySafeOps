The chase is cut indoors with a petrol saw, no water, door closed for "warmth". The brickie wears a dust mask from last month's job, beard untrimmed, mask not sealed. Nobody has a [COSHH assessment](/blog/coshh-register-software-uk) for that task on this shift. That scene repeats on UK housing, civils and refit sites every week.

**Silica dust** is not new. HSE attention is. Respirable crystalline silica (RCS) from cutting, grinding, drilling and chasing masonry products is a focus of inspection campaigns and client audits in 2026. The controls are known: stop the dust at source, ventilate, respirators as last resort, record what you did.

This guide covers what UK site managers need for silica on construction sites, and how briefings tie to [digital toolbox talks](/blog/digital-toolbox-talks) and [HSE inspection prep](/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk).

## Where silica appears on UK sites

Common tasks generating RCS:

| Task | Typical material |
| --- | --- |
| Cutting paving / kerbs | Concrete, sandstone |
| Chasing brick block walls | Clay brick, concrete block |
| Core drilling | Concrete, stone |
| Grinding joints | Mortar, tile |
| Demolition | Unknown legacy materials (assume RCS until surveyed) |
| Kitchen/stone worktops | Quartz engineered stone (very high silica content) |

Engineered stone worktops have driven renewed HSE focus. Standard UK housing and civils still generate huge exposure from quick cuts without water.

## Health effects and WEL

RCS causes silicosis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and lung cancer. Effects are long latency; young operatives feel fine today.

Under COSHH, the workplace exposure limit (WEL) for respirable crystalline silica is **0.1 mg/m³** 8-hour TWA. You should not rely on RPE alone to hit the WEL. Engineering controls first.

Assess exposure when methods change: enclosed spaces, winter enclosed cutting, night shifts with poor ventilation.

## Hierarchy of control for RCS

**1. Eliminate / substitute**
Precast openings, fewer on-site cuts, alternative materials where specification allows.

**2. Engineering controls**
- **Water suppression** on saws and grinders (wet cutting)
- **On-tool extraction** (M-class vacuum, hose to tool)
- **Enclosure** with negative pressure for fixed cutting stations

**3. Administrative**
- Limit cutting hours, job rotation
- Permit for enclosed high-dust work ([permit to work](/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk) where your system uses it)
- [Toolbox talk](/blog/digital-toolbox-talks) before task starts

**4. RPE**
FFP3 or powered respirator where exposure remains significant. **Face fit test** required for tight-fitting masks. No beards with tight-fitting RPE unless you use loose-fitting powered hoods.

**5. Welfare**
Do not blow dust off clothes with airline. Vac or wet wipe. Separate eating area away from cutting.

Summer heat makes enclosed cutting worse; pair silica controls with [heat safety](/blog/summer-heat-safety-construction-uk).

## RPE and face fit

Common failures:

- FFP2 used where FFP3 needed
- No face fit certificate on file
- Same mask for multiple workers
- Mask in pocket until inspector arrives

Record training and fit test dates. Store SDS for consumables in your [COSHH register](/blog/coshh-register-software-uk).

## COSHH records and health surveillance

COSHH requires:

- **Suitable and sufficient assessment** for silica-generating tasks
- **Control measures** identified and maintained
- **Information and training** to workers
- **Health surveillance** where exposure risk remains despite controls (lung function / questionnaire programmes arranged with occupational health)

Digital records help when HSE asks what was briefed on the day of a visit. Paper assessments in a drawer dated 2019 do not.

## What HSE checks on site

Typical inspection lines:

- Cutting method (wet vs dry)
- Extraction equipment maintained and used
- RPE type, storage, fit testing records
- COSHH assessment available for the task
- Workers can describe controls (not just wear mask for camera)
- Enclosed areas: ventilation and exclusion

Silica often appears in the same visit as welfare, [height](/blog/height-work-permit-uk), [asbestos](/blog/asbestos-management-construction-uk), and [noise at work](/blog/noise-at-work-construction-uk) wider checks.

Link silica planning to [RAMS writing](/blog/how-to-write-a-rams-uk): method statement should name suppression, RPE, and waste cleanup.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is silica dust illegal on UK construction sites?**
The work is legal with proper controls. Uncontrolled exposure breaches COSHH and WEL requirements.

**Is wet cutting enough?**
Often wet cutting is the main control, but assess enclosed spaces and duration. Add extraction or RPE where needed.

**Do I need face fit testing for dust masks?**
Yes for tight-fitting FFP3 used as exposure control. Keep certificates.

**What about engineered stone worktops?**
Very high silica risk. HSE has emphasised stricter controls; treat as high priority assessment.

**Should silica be in toolbox talks?**
Yes when cutting, chasing, or demolition is on the programme. Record attendance.

**How does silica relate to COSHH registers?**
Cutting discs, additives, and stone products may have SDS. Link assessments to substances and tasks in your register.
