The client wants work to start Monday. The principal contractor downloads a free **Construction Phase Plan template**, fills the cover page, leaves half the hazard sections as "N/A", and files it in the site cabin. By Wednesday the excavation subcontractor is on site, the crane laydown has moved, and nobody has updated the traffic plan. The CPP still describes last month's layout.

CDM 2015 Regulation 12 requires a Construction Phase Plan for every construction project. On **notifiable projects**, the plan must be prepared before construction work begins. The CPP is how the principal contractor shows they have thought about how the site will be run safely, not just how each trade will do its own job. For Reg 12-14 duties in plain English, see [principal contractor duties UK](/blog/principal-contractor-duties-uk).

This guide explains what a UK **CPP template** should contain, what HSE and clients actually look for, and how digital plans stay aligned with live site conditions. For the wider software picture, see [CDM 2015 compliance software](/blog/cdm-2015-compliance-software-uk).

## When you need a Construction Phase Plan

| Project type | Who prepares CPP | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Notifiable** (see [F10 rules](/blog/f10-notification-uk)) | Principal contractor | Before work starts on site |
| **Non-notifiable** | Contractor in control (often same firm as PC) | Before work starts |
| **Small contractor job** | Contractor | Simplified plan still required |

Domestic clients still trigger CDM duties above notification thresholds. Small firms can use a shorter plan; see [CDM 2015 for small contractors](/blog/cdm-2015-small-contractor-uk).

The CPP must be **proportionate**. A house extension template pasted onto a hospital refurbishment will fail a client audit. Scale sections to risk.

## CPP vs project RAMS

| | Construction Phase Plan | Subcontractor RAMS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Principal contractor | Each contractor |
| **Scope** | Whole site management | Specific trade activity |
| **Covers** | Welfare, traffic, fire, coordination | Method, tools, trade hazards |
| **Updates** | When site layout or programme changes | When method changes |

The CPP references significant risks and how they are coordinated. It does not replace a steel erector's RAMS or a scaffolder's design. It should say how those RAMS are checked, briefed and linked to [permits](/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk) where needed.

## What sections belong in the template

HSE's CPP structure (Construction Phase Plan checklist) is the baseline most UK PCs follow. A workable template includes:

**1. Project description and management structure**
Client, PD, PC, site manager, H&S contact, emergency numbers. Organisation chart for larger sites.

**2. Site rules**
PPE baseline, smoking, phones, working alone, out-of-hours rules, subbie sign-in.

**3. Welfare arrangements**
Toilets, rest areas, water, drying rooms, first aid provision, mental health signposting where relevant.

**4. Fire and emergency**
Alarm, muster points, extinguishers, hot work rules, liaison with neighbours or hospital sites if applicable.

**5. Traffic and pedestrian management**
Delivery routes, reversing rules, banksman requirements, exclusion zones, interface with public highways.

**6. Significant site risks**
Work at height strategy, excavation, temporary works, lifting, asbestos (if survey exists), confined spaces, overhead power. Cross-reference to [height permits](/blog/height-work-permit-uk), [excavation permits](/blog/excavation-permit-uk) and trade RAMS.

**7. Contractor coordination**
How RAMS are collected, induction delivered ([site induction software](/blog/site-induction-software-uk) or paper), toolbox talk frequency, permit system.

**8. Monitoring and review**
Who reviews the CPP, when (e.g. monthly or at programme stage gates), how changes are communicated.

## Notifiable vs non-notifiable projects

**Notifiable projects** face stricter scrutiny. Clients and HSE expect:

- F10 submitted before start
- CPP in place before first operative arrives
- Principal designer cooperation recorded
- Pre-construction information considered in the plan

**Non-notifiable** projects still need a CPP, but it can be shorter. A bathroom rip-out by a single contractor might be ten pages covering induction, dust, isolation and waste, not a traffic management chapter.

Do not confuse **notifiable** with **high risk**. A long low-risk refurbishment can be notifiable on duration alone.

## Common CPP failures on UK sites

- **Generic template, no site specifics:** "Traffic management per drawing" with no drawing attached.
- **Never updated after mobilisation:** laydown moved, CPP did not.
- **RAMS dump:** 400 pages of subbie PDFs labelled as the CPP.
- **Missing welfare detail:** inspectors notice toilets before they notice your risk matrix.
- **No link to permits:** hot work happening with no mention of fire watch in the CPP.
- **Designer residual risks ignored:** structural temporary works absent from section 6.

During [HSE inspection season](/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk), the CPP is usually the first document requested alongside induction records.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there an official HSE Construction Phase Plan template?**
HSE provides guidance and checklist content rather than a single mandatory form. Use their structure; tailor to your project.

**Who writes the CPP on a small job?**
The contractor in control of the work. On notifiable projects, the principal contractor must prepare it.

**How long should a Construction Phase Plan be?**
As long as needed to cover site management arrangements proportionately. Ten pages can suffice for small work; large civils projects run longer.

**When should the CPP be updated?**
When site layout, programme, or significant methods change, or after an incident that exposes a gap.

**Does the CPP need every subcontractor RAMS attached?**
No. The CPP should explain how RAMS are managed and coordinated, not duplicate every method statement.

**Can I use the same CPP on two sites?**
Each site needs its own plan. Reuse structure and boilerplate, but site rules, traffic, welfare and hazards must match each location.
