*By the MySafeOps team · Last updated 8 July 2026 · 8 min read*

> - The Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005 apply to every UK construction site.
> - Lower exposure action value: 80 dB(A); upper: 85 dB(A); exposure limit: 87 dB(A).

The breaker runs for four hours in a basement car park. The operative wears ear defenders bought from a market stall. Nobody measured noise. Nobody rotated the job. Ten years on, tinnitus is permanent. The RAMS said "wear PPE" and nothing else.

**Noise at work** on UK construction sites is normalised. Grinders, breakers, generators, steel fixing guns, and plant backup alarms sit above exposure action values daily. The Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005 apply on every notifiable project under **CDM 2015** as well as general health and safety law.

This guide sits alongside [silica dust controls](/blog/silica-dust-construction-uk), [HSE inspection prep](/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk), and [RAMS writing](/blog/how-to-write-a-rams-uk).

## Why construction noise matters long term

Noise-induced hearing loss is irreversible. Tinnitus affects sleep and mental health. Claims and surveillance costs follow.

High-noise tasks on UK sites:

| Task | Typical concern |
| --- | --- |
| Breakers / demolition | Very high levels, long duration |
| Cutting / grinding | High, close to head |
| Pile driving / percussive plant | Area exposure |
| Generators in enclosed spaces | Reflection increases dose |
| Shooting pins / nail guns | Impulse noise |

## Legal limits in plain English

Daily or weekly exposure (8-hour reference):

| Level | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **80 dB(A) lower action value** | Provide hearing protection on request; information and training |
| **85 dB(A) upper action value** | Hearing protection mandatory in hearing protection zones; reduce exposure |
| **87 dB(A) exposure limit** | Must not be exceeded (accounting for PPE attenuation) |

Peak sound pressure limit: **140 dB(C)**.

Employers must assess exposure. "We always wear defenders" is not an assessment.

## Hierarchy of noise control on site

**1. Eliminate / substitute**
Quiet work methods, precast instead of on-site cutting, hydraulic instead of percussive where possible.

**2. Engineering**
Quieter plant, barriers, screens, mufflers maintained, generators positioned away from work faces.

**3. Administrative**
- **Job rotation** so no one spends full shift on breaker
- **Programme sequencing**: noisy work when fewer people on site
- **Signage** for hearing protection zones
- Brief in [toolbox talks](/blog/digital-toolbox-talks)

**4. PPE**
Ear defenders or plugs with suitable SNR. Train fit and use. PPE is last, not first.

## Hearing protection and zones

Mark zones on plan in the [Construction Phase Plan](/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk). Brief at induction ([site induction software](/blog/site-induction-software-uk)).

Failures:

- Defenders on forehead, not ears
- No zones, so visitors walk into breaker area unprotected
- Incompatible RPE: tight mask and defender headband together (see [silica](/blog/silica-dust-construction-uk) dual PPE planning)

## Health surveillance and records

When exposure is at or above **85 dB(A)**, provide **audiometry** health surveillance unless risk assessment shows it is not required.

Keep:

- Noise risk assessment and measurements
- Training records
- Surveillance results (under medical confidentiality)
- Evidence of tool rotation and planning

Digital records help when clients audit alongside [subcontractor management](/blog/subcontractor-management-software-uk).

## What HSE checks

During [HSE inspection season](/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk):

- Is there a noise risk assessment?
- Are breakers used without rotation?
- Hearing protection zones signed?
- Surveillance in place for high-exposure roles?
- Quieter methods considered?

Noise often appears with dust and welfare checks on the same walk-round.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the legal noise limit on UK construction sites?**
The exposure limit value is 87 dB(A) daily/weekly exposure, not exceeded even with PPE. Action values start at 80 and 85 dB(A).

**Is hearing protection enough?**
No. You must reduce noise at source first, then use PPE where exposure remains high.

**Do small sites need noise assessments?**
Yes, proportionate to risk. A day with a breaker in a basement still needs assessment and controls.

**When is health surveillance required?**
Typically when exposure is at or above the upper action value (85 dB(A)), unless assessment justifies otherwise.

**How does noise relate to RAMS?**
Method statements should name noisy steps, rotation, zones, and PPE. See [how to write a RAMS UK](/blog/how-to-write-a-rams-uk).

**Can noise affect lone workers?**
Yes. [Lone workers](/blog/lone-worker-construction-uk) in plant rooms may have higher exposure with nobody to share rotation.
