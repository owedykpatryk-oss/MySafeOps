A load slips on the crane hook and drops six inches onto the exclusion zone. Nobody hurt. Someone laughs it off. Two weeks later the same set-up fails for real. The investigation asks why the first event was never logged.

**Near miss reporting** on UK construction sites is not bureaucracy for its own sake. It is how you find weak controls before [RIDDOR](/blog/riddor-changes-2026) becomes the record. HSE and clients increasingly ask principal contractors how they learn from close calls under **CDM 2015**.

## What counts as a near miss

Useful definition for site:

> An unplanned event that did not result in injury, illness or damage, but had the potential to do so.

Examples:

- Fall of materials that missed people
- Operative steps onto an unguarded edge and recovers
- Hot work spark lands outside the permit area, extinguished quickly
- Vehicle and pedestrian almost collide at the gate
- Incorrect isolation discovered before work started

Do not argue taxonomy for twenty minutes. If it scared a competent person, log it. Minor property damage and unsafe conditions can sit in the same system with clear categories.

Contrast with accidents and [dangerous occurrences](/blog/riddor-changes-2026) that may need formal reporting. Near misses feed prevention; RIDDOR covers statutory duties when thresholds are met.

## Why paper pads fail on site

Classic failure mode:

1. Pad in the cabin
2. Ganger does not walk back to write it up
3. Supervisor finds a blank page at month end
4. Client audit asks for learning actions — nothing to show

Problems:

- Language and literacy barriers for some operatives
- Fear of being blamed for "making a fuss"
- No photo, location or permit reference
- Actions never assigned or closed
- Subbies excluded from the pad ([subcontractor management](/blog/subcontractor-management-software-uk))

Mobile reporting with free worker accounts removes the walk-back problem. That is why adoption matters more than a glossy policy PDF.

## How to run a simple reporting system

Minimum viable process:

| Step | Owner | Done looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capture | Anyone on site | Photo, location, what almost happened, time |
| Triage | Supervisor / H&S | Severity, who else needs to know today |
| Action | Named person | Control change, repair, briefing, permit update |
| Close | Supervisor | Evidence action done; share with relevant trades |
| Trend | PC / H&S | Monthly themes into CPP and RAMS reviews |

Rules that work:

- Report in under two minutes
- No punishment for good-faith reports
- Feedback within a shift for serious events
- Link to open [permits](/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk) and work package where relevant

## Investigate without the blame circus

Serious near misses need a short investigation. Keep it proportionate:

1. What was supposed to happen (RAMS / permit)
2. What actually happened
3. What failed (guard, exclusion, competence, weather, communication)
4. What changes now

Avoid "who was careless" as the first question. Ask why the system allowed the miss. That aligns with how [HSE inspections](/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk) look at management arrangements, not only individual error.

Share findings in a [toolbox talk](/blog/digital-toolbox-talks) or induction refresh when the learning applies site-wide.

## Link reports to RAMS, permits and CPP

Closing the loop means changing controls:

- Update the [RAMS](/blog/how-to-write-a-rams-uk) if the method was wrong
- Suspend or amend live [permits](/blog/digital-permit-board-construction-uk) if conditions changed
- Fix access, lighting, or pedestrian routes after vehicle near misses ([site security / access](/blog/construction-site-security-uk))
- Feed recurring themes into the [Construction Phase Plan](/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk)

If the same near miss repeats three times, you do not have a reporting problem — you have an action problem.

## Metrics that matter

Count reports carefully. A rising number often means trust is up, not that the site got worse.

Better measures:

- % of reports with closed actions within agreed time
- Repeat themes month on month
- Time from report to briefing of affected trades
- Subcontractor participation rate

Do not set a target of "zero near misses". That kills honesty. Target closed actions and fewer repeats.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is near miss reporting a legal requirement?**
There is no single form called "near miss" in law. Employers must manage risks and learn from incidents. Clients and principal contractors often require a formal system under CDM arrangements and contracts.

**What is the difference between a near miss and RIDDOR?**
Near misses did not cause reportable harm. RIDDOR covers specified injuries, diseases and dangerous occurrences that meet legal thresholds. Some near misses sit next to dangerous occurrences — get competent advice when unsure.

**Should reports be anonymous?**
Optional anonymity can increase reporting. You still need enough detail to act. Named reports help follow-up when trust exists.

**How fast should we investigate?**
Serious potential: same shift. Lower potential: within a few days. Always close actions and tell people what changed.

**Do agency workers and visitors report too?**
Yes. Include them in induction. Visitor and gate near misses often reveal access failures — see [visitor management](/blog/construction-site-visitor-management-uk).

**How does this link to permits?**
Many near misses happen during permitted work. Reference the permit number in the report and update the [digital permit board](/blog/digital-permit-board-construction-uk) if controls change.
