Saturday 02:30. The site alarm calls the duty manager. Someone has cut the hoarding behind the laydown, loaded copper cable onto a van, and driven off before security arrives. The CCTV was pointing at the gate, not the weak panel. The visitor book in the cabin still shows Friday's deliveries signed in, but nobody signed out.

**Construction site security** in the UK is usually discussed as theft prevention. It is also trespass control, arson reduction, evidence preservation after incidents, and proof that you managed access under CDM. Empty sites over summer weekends are soft targets. Active sites with weak gates leak tools daily.

This guide covers practical fencing, access control, and how security ties to [visitor management](/blog/construction-site-visitor-management-uk) and the [Construction Phase Plan](/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk).

## Why site security matters beyond theft

| Risk | Typical trigger |
| --- | --- |
| **Plant and tool theft** | Open laydown, no CCTV, weak locks |
| **Material loss** | Copper, fuel, batteries, small plant |
| **Trespass and vandalism** | Gaps in hoarding, urban sites |
| **Arson** | Combustible waste, unsecured cabins |
| **Injury to trespassers** | You still face questions on access control |
| **Data and drawings** | Cabin break-in, unlocked laptops |

Insurance may not cover everything. Programme slips when a stolen telehandler stops pours. Clients audit security on rail, hospital, and defence jobs.

## Legal and CDM context

CDM 2015 expects sites to **prevent unauthorised access**. That sits with the principal contractor's site rules in the CPP. HSE also publishes guidance on securing sites.

Security overlaps:

- **Regulation 13/14** access control ([principal contractor duties](/blog/principal-contractor-duties-uk))
- **Visitor records** for authorised people ([visitor management UK](/blog/construction-site-visitor-management-uk))
- **Fire watch** after [hot work](/blog/hot-work-permit-uk): open sites undo permit controls

During [HSE inspection season](/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk), inspectors notice open gates, missing hoarding, and cabins with no lock.

## Perimeter and access controls

Baseline measures most UK principal contractors specify:

**Hoarding and fencing**
- 2.4 m hoarding on public boundaries where required by planning
- Internal mesh fencing between zones (live work / public interface)
- Daily walk to check panels after high wind

**Controlled gates**
- Single manned gate where possible; turnstiles on large civils
- Delivery booking slots linked to sign-in
- No "propped open for convenience" without a written rule

**Lighting and CCTV**
- Cover laydown and plant, not just the gate portrait shot
- ANPR or motion alerts on remote sites
- GDPR notice for CCTV (overlap with visitor privacy)

**Keys and codes**
- Cabin keys, compound codes: issue log, recover on demob
- Subbie compounds keyed separately where thefts are recurring

## Plant, tools and materials

- **Plant immobilisers** and tracked GPS on telehandlers where budget allows
- **Tool vaults** in cabins; no overnight loose batteries on charge in open containers
- **Marked assets** and weekly plant reconciliations
- **Fuel bowsers** locked; spill kits separate from waste skips (arson risk)
- **High-value materials** delivery just-in-time rather than week-long copper stacks

Coordinate with [subcontractor management](/blog/subcontractor-management-software-uk): each trade secures its own kit, PC audits compounds.

## Out-of-hours and weekend cover

Summer programme peaks leave sites quiet Sunday-Tuesday. Controls:

- **Patrol contract** or neighbour liaison on high-risk urban sites
- **Alarm response** tested monthly
- **Hot work close-out** verified before last person leaves ([digital permit board](/blog/digital-permit-board-construction-uk) helps)
- **Waste and packaging** cleared: arson fuel
- **Social media discipline**: crews posting crane views reveal layout

Link weekend security briefings to [digital toolbox talks](/blog/digital-toolbox-talks) when gangs return Monday.

## Linking security to visitor management

Security fails when delivery drivers, client reps, and subbie supervisors use different sign-in systems.

One approach:

1. **All non-inducted people** sign visitor log at gate
2. **Escort rules** for active work areas
3. **Sign-out** enforced for fire roll call accuracy
4. **Photo ID** on long projects where card sharing happens
5. **Night access list** agreed with security contractor

## Frequently asked questions

**Is construction site security a legal requirement in the UK?**
CDM requires preventing unauthorised access. Security measures are how PCs demonstrate that duty.

**What fencing height is standard on UK sites?**
Planning conditions often specify 2.4 m hoarding on public boundaries. Internal zones may use lower mesh fencing.

**Who is responsible for site security?**
The principal contractor sets site rules in the CPP. Subcontractors secure their own compounds within those rules.

**Does CCTV on site need GDPR compliance?**
Yes. Signage, lawful basis, retention limits, and access requests apply.

**How do we reduce plant theft?**
Immobilisers, GPS, locked compounds, lighting, CCTV on laydown, and delivery timing for high-value materials.

**Should security be in the Construction Phase Plan?**
Yes. Include perimeter, gates, out-of-hours arrangements, and how visitors are controlled.
