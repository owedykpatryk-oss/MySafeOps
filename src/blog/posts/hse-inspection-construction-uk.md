The inspector arrives at 10:15 without an appointment. The site manager is on a MEWP briefing, the cabin is locked, and the fire watch sheet for yesterday's welding is still in the welder's van. The HSE officer waits at the gate, hi-vis on, clipboard ready. That first ten minutes sets the tone for everything that follows.

Summer is **HSE inspection season** on UK construction sites. Activity ramps up, agency labour increases, and HSE runs national campaigns on work at height, respiratory risk, mental health and sector-specific hazards. You will not always get notice. What you can control is whether your records and live controls match what your RAMS say.

This guide is for site managers, H&S advisers and principal contractors who want to pass an inspection without a scramble: and understand what inspectors actually look for when they walk your job.

## When HSE inspection season hits UK sites

HSE does not publish a single "inspection fortnight" for all construction, but **May through September** consistently sees higher inspection activity. Factors:

- **Weather**: more external work at height, excavation and roofing.
- **Programme pressure**: clients push summer completions; shortcuts show up in permits and inductions.
- **Campaign topics**: HSE announces focus areas (recent years have included [silica](/blog/silica-dust-construction-uk), mental health, and work at height). Campaigns drive targeted visits.
- **Incident data**: sectors with rising RIDDOR reports attract attention. See [RIDDOR changes 2026](/blog/riddor-changes-2026) if your reporting processes are under review.

Not every visit is a campaign. HSE also responds to complaints, reports of dangerous occurrences, and follow-ups from previous enforcement. Treat every month as inspection-ready, not just July.

## What triggers a visit

| Trigger | What it means for your site |
| --- | --- |
| **National campaign** | Sector-wide focus; your trade may be on the list even if you had no incidents |
| **RIDDOR report** | Follow-up visit likely; records from the day of the incident will be examined |
| **Worker or public complaint** | Often welfare, dust, or work at height; inspector may arrive unannounced |
| **Previous enforcement notice** | Revisit to check compliance; paperwork must show closure |
| **Notifiable project (F10)** | HSE knows the project exists; larger sites face proportionate attention |
| **Fatality or major injury nearby** | Increased scrutiny on similar activities in the region |

You cannot opt out. You can make the visit boring: in a good way: by having documents ready and hazards controlled.

## Documents inspectors ask for first

Expect requests for:

**CDM and project files**
- F10 confirmation (if notifiable)
- Construction Phase Plan: current version
- Pre-construction information
- Principal designer / principal contractor appointments

**People records**
- Site induction register: names, dates, signatures or digital acknowledgements
- Training evidence for specialised work (IPAF, CISRS, confined space, [asbestos awareness](/blog/asbestos-management-construction-uk))
- [Toolbox talk records](/blog/digital-toolbox-talks) for recent weeks

**Risk assessments and permits**
- RAMS for activities in progress
- Active [permits to work](/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk): hot work, height, excavation, [electrical isolation](/blog/electrical-isolation-permit-uk)
- Closed permits for work in the last 7–14 days (inspectors check fire watch close-out)

**Equipment and registers**
- [Scaffold inspection tags](/blog/scaffold-inspection-checklist-uk) and weekly register
- LOLER thorough examination records for lifting equipment; see [lifting operations permit UK](/blog/lifting-operations-permit-uk) for site authorisation
- [COSHH register](/blog/coshh-register-software-uk) and SDS for substances in use

**Incident and reporting**
- Accident book entries
- RIDDOR decision log (reportable or not, with reasoning)
- Near-miss records if your policy captures them

Paper systems can work if the cabin is organised. Digital systems win when the inspector asks for "last Tuesday's hot work permit" and you pull it in thirty seconds.

## Physical checks on the live site

After paperwork, the inspector walks the job. Frequent focus areas:

**Work at height**: guardrails, scaffold tags, MEWP ground conditions, fragile roof controls. Tie to [height work permits](/blog/height-work-permit-uk) if your system uses them.

**Excavation**: shoring, batter angles, utility detection records, exclusion zones. See [excavation permit UK](/blog/excavation-permit-uk) for what a permit should capture.

**Hot work**: fire watch duration, combustibles cleared, gas cylinders stored correctly. Open permits must match what is happening at the torch.

**Respiratory and noise**: [silica](/blog/silica-dust-construction-uk), [asbestos disturbance](/blog/asbestos-management-construction-uk), wet cutting, extraction, hearing protection zones. See [noise at work](/blog/noise-at-work-construction-uk) for exposure limits.

**Lone working**: night security, plant rooms, empty floors. Check risk assessments and check-in records. See [lone working on construction sites](/blog/lone-worker-construction-uk).

**Welfare**: toilets, drinking water, rest areas. Underrated cause of improvement notices on smaller sites. In summer, heat stress and shade also draw attention; see our [summer heat safety guide](/blog/summer-heat-safety-construction-uk).

**Housekeeping**: trip hazards, waste fires, pedestrian/vehicle segregation. Poor housekeeping suggests poor management.

**PPE**: inspectors notice missing helmets, but management failures (no induction, no permit, no edge protection) carry more weight than one operative without gloves.

## Common failures that lead to enforcement

- **No F10 on a notifiable project**: easy win for inspectors; suggests wider CDM gaps.
- **Induction gaps for agency staff**: names on the gate list but not in the register.
- **RAMS generic, site specific risk live**: boilerplate method statement while the actual edge has no protection.
- **Permit signed, control not in place**: the classic "paper safe, site unsafe" finding.
- **Fire watch not closed out**: hot work permit still open days later, or no record of the 60-minute watch.
- **Scaffold tag expired**: seven-day cycle missed after a bank holiday weekend.
- **No thorough examination certificate** for the telehandler on site that morning.

Enforcement scales from verbal advice through improvement notices to prohibition notices and prosecution. The sites that fare worst combine a serious live hazard with missing records. Fix the records and the live hazard before July programme pressure peaks.

## How to prepare in 48 hours

If you suspect a visit or you are on a campaign trade list:

**Day 1: documents**
1. Confirm F10 submitted if required.
2. Check CPP matches current phase of work; update if stale.
3. Reconcile gate register with induction list.
4. Pull active permits; close or extend anything orphaned.
5. Verify scaffold and LOLER paperwork on site matches tagged equipment.

**Day 2: site walk**
1. Work at height and excavation areas: match RAMS and permits.
2. Hot work areas: combustibles, extinguishers, fire watch names.
3. Welfare and housekeeping: quick wins before an inspector does the same walk.
4. Brief supervisors: honest answers, fetch documents promptly, do not guess.

Run a **mock inspection** with your H&S adviser. Ask for the same documents HSE will. Gaps found internally are cheaper than gaps found on a prohibition notice.

| Paper-based site | Digital-ready site |
| --- | --- |
| 20–45 minutes finding files | Records on tablet in minutes |
| Risk of missing closed permits | Search by date, trade, location |
| Agency inductions lag behind attendance | Same-day induction before turnstile |
| Hard to prove fire watch close-out | Timestamped permit lifecycle |

## Frequently asked questions

**Do HSE inspectors give notice before a construction site visit?**
Sometimes, but many inspections are unannounced. Assume no notice.

**What is the first thing HSE asks for on a construction site?**
Often the Construction Phase Plan, induction records and RAMS for work in progress. On notifiable projects, F10 confirmation is standard.

**Can HSE shut down a construction site?**
Yes. Prohibition notices stop specified work or whole sites until serious risks are fixed. Improvement notices allow work to continue with a deadline to comply.

**How long does a construction site HSE inspection take?**
From under an hour for a focused visit to a full day on large notifiable projects with multiple subcontractors.

**Does good paperwork matter if the site looks safe?**
Yes. CDM requires management arrangements to be documented. Inspectors use records to judge whether safety is systematic or lucky.

**What should subcontractors prepare for a principal contractor HSE visit?**
Their own RAMS, permits, tool box talks, training cards and induction evidence for their operatives. PCs aggregate CDM duty; each firm still owns its method.
