# Lone Working on UK Construction Sites: Risk Assessment and Check-In

*By the MySafeOps team · Last updated 8 July 2026 · 8 min read*

> **Key takeaways**
> - Lone working is not banned in UK construction, but risk must be controlled first.
> - High-risk tasks (confined space, height, live electrics) should not be done alone.
> - Check-in systems need a named responder who will act if someone misses a call.
> - CPP site rules should state when lone work is permitted and what tech is used.
> - Records prove the assessment existed before an incident, not after.

The security guard does rounds alone at 22:00. The spark tests circuits alone in the plant room. The cleaner works the empty floor with no check-in. Monday morning someone asks "who was last to see him?" and the cabin has no log.

**Lone working** on UK construction sites is common on security, snagging, commissioning, and small maintenance tasks. It is not illegal. It is high risk when the task, environment, or medical factors mean one person cannot raise the alarm or self-rescue.

HSE expects proportionate controls. Clients on rail, hospital, and defence jobs often specify lone worker systems in site rules.

## What lone working means on site

Lone working means people cannot be seen or heard by colleagues, not necessarily that they are the only person on the whole project.

Examples:

- Night security patrol
- Single operative in basement plant room
- Surveyor on empty floor
- Driver delivering materials (brief lone period)
- Remote civils operative on long pipe run

Contrast with a roofer alone on a deck while ground crew are nearby: still risky, but not the same isolation profile.

## When lone work is unacceptable

Do not allow lone work for:

| Activity | Why |
| --- | --- |
| [Confined space entry](https://mysafeops.com/blog/confined-space-permit-uk) | Statutory standby and rescue |
| [Work at height](https://mysafeops.com/blog/height-work-permit-uk) without fall protection | Rescue and witness |
| [Live electrical work](https://mysafeops.com/blog/electrical-isolation-permit-uk) | Proving dead needs second person often |
| [Hot work](https://mysafeops.com/blog/hot-work-permit-uk) without fire watch | Fire watch cannot be same person |
| Heavy lifting / MEWP | Emergency lowering |
| Asbestos disturbance | Supervision and clearance |

If the method needs a standby, it is not lone work. Call it what it is in the RAMS.

## Risk assessment essentials

Document:

- **Task and location** (including access routes)
- **Duration** and time of day
- **Communication** (mobile signal tested on site)
- **Medical factors** (heart conditions, diabetes: occupational health input)
- **Violence risk** on public-facing or vacant buildings ([site security](https://mysafeops.com/blog/construction-site-security-uk))
- **Environment** (heat, [noise](https://mysafeops.com/blog/noise-at-work-construction-uk), fumes)
- **Emergency plan** (who responds, how fast, first aid)

Review when task, building state, or weather changes.

## Check-in and monitoring systems

Options:

- **Phone check-in** at set intervals with escalation
- **Lone worker apps** with man-down and GPS (privacy policy required)
- **Buddy system** visual checks on same floor
- **Access control logs** from turnstile ([visitor management](https://mysafeops.com/blog/construction-site-visitor-management-uk))

Rules that work:

1. Named **monitor** on shift with authority to send help
2. **Escalation path** if two checks missed
3. **Test signal** where work happens, not at gate only
4. **Out-of-hours number** that reaches a human

## Linking lone work to permits and CPP

The [Construction Phase Plan](https://mysafeops.com/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk) should state:

- Lone work permitted / not permitted by default
- Check-in interval and monitor role
- High-risk permits that prohibit lone work
- Night work and security interface

[Digital permit boards](https://mysafeops.com/blog/digital-permit-board-construction-uk) should show live permits so monitors know hot work or height is active while someone is alone elsewhere.

Principal contractors coordinate this under [CDM 2015](https://mysafeops.com/blog/principal-contractor-duties-uk) Regulation 13 duties.

## Common failures

- **Lone work by culture** ("he always works alone")
- **No signal** in plant rooms; app useless
- **Check-in to voicemail**
- **Security alone** without violence assessment on empty sites
- **No linkage** between lone worker policy and [PTW](https://mysafeops.com/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is lone working illegal in UK construction?**
No, but you must assess and control risks. Some tasks should never be done alone.

**What check-in interval should we use?**
Risk-based. Higher risk, shorter interval. Document in risk assessment.

**Do lone worker apps satisfy HSE?**
They help if monitored and tested on site. They do not replace task risk controls.

**Who monitors lone workers on site?**
A named competent person on shift with authority to escalate and send help.

**Should lone working be in site induction?**
Yes. Operatives should know check-in rules and prohibited lone tasks.

**How does lone working relate to permits?**
Permits for confined space, hot work, height and isolation should require more than one person. Lone work policy must align.
