It is 2 pm in July. The roofer's foreman has been on black membrane all day. He feels sick, blames lunch, keeps working. His mate notices slurred speech and calls the supervisor. By then core temperature is already in dangerous territory.

UK construction still treats sun as a bonus. But **heat stress** sends operatives home, triggers RIDDOR reports in bad cases, and lands firms in HSE conversations during summer inspection campaigns. You do not need a Mediterranean climate for heat to bite. Reflective roofs, excavations with no breeze, and tyvek suits turn a 28°C day into something worse.

This guide covers what site managers and H&S leads should do on **UK construction sites** when temperatures rise, without pretending there is a single legal maximum temperature (there is not). For inspection readiness, pair this with [HSE inspection season](https://mysafeops.com/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk).

## Why UK sites underestimate heat

- **PPE does not switch off in July:** hi-vis, helmets, harnesses and FR clothing trap heat.
- **Programme pressure:** summer is when clients want roofs closed and roads reopened.
- **Agency labour:** new starters are less acclimatised to heavy work in heat.
- **"Just drink water"** is not a plan if there is no shade and no rest.

HSE thermal comfort guidance treats heat as a risk to assess, like noise or dust. Management arrangements should cover hot conditions in your Construction Phase Plan or site rules. See [CPP template UK](https://mysafeops.com/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk) for where heat fits in site-wide planning.

## Signs of heat stress and heat stroke

**Early heat stress:** headache, dizziness, cramps, heavy sweating, confusion, irritability.

**Heat exhaustion:** pale skin, nausea, rapid pulse, still sweating but weakening.

**Heat stroke (emergency):** hot dry skin or altered sweating, confusion, collapse, seizures. Call 999. Cool immediately while waiting.

Supervisors need a one-page aide memoire in the cabin. First aiders should know heat stroke is not "sit in the shade with a Coke".

## Practical controls that work on site

**Water**
Chilled where possible, unlimited refills, cups or bottles workers will actually use. One lukewarm bowser 200 m from the work face does not count.

**Shade and rest areas**
Marquees, cabins, or north-facing hoarding. Rest is not weakness on a 35°C roof day.

**Reschedule heavy work**
Pour concrete, steel erection, and ground compaction at start/end of day when feasible. Programme realistically in heatwaves.

**Acclimatisation**
New starters and returnees from leave need lighter duties for the first few hot days.

**Ventilation**
Enclosed spaces and plant cabins need airflow. Check [confined space](https://mysafeops.com/blog/confined-space-permit-uk) arrangements double in heat.

**Skin protection**
Sun cream stations, encourage sleeves where compatible with risk. Sunburn increases dehydration risk.

## Work-rest cycles and programme shifts

There is no single UK statutory work-rest table for construction heat, unlike some international mining rules. Use risk assessment:

- Increase breaks when Met Office heat-health alerts are active
- Rotate trades on exposed elevations
- Pair experienced supervisors with agency gangs who may not speak up when ill

Document the trigger for enhanced breaks: e.g. "when site thermometer in work area exceeds 30°C" or "when Met Office amber heat alert". Consistency beats ad hoc sympathy.

Hot work adds ignition risk when vegetation dries. Tie heat planning to [hot work permits](https://mysafeops.com/blog/hot-work-permit-uk) and fire watch capacity.

## PPE, hydration and welfare

**Welfare regulations** already require drinking water and rest facilities. Heat makes enforcement visible: inspectors check toilets, water, and shade in the same walk as edge protection.

**PPE compromise:** where FR clothing is mandatory, compensate with more frequent breaks and hydration. Do not drop FR for comfort.

**Caffeine and energy drinks** dehydrate. Briefings should say so plainly.

**Lone workers** on plant or remote civils need check-ins more often in heat.

## Recording briefings and audits

If heat controls are in your RAMS, brief when conditions change. Record:

- Date and time of briefing
- Topic (heat stress, water points, rest rules)
- Attendees or acknowledgements
- Supervisor name

Paper registers work. [Digital toolbox talks](https://mysafeops.com/blog/digital-toolbox-talks) help when HSE or a client asks what you told agency staff on the hottest Tuesday in July.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a maximum temperature to stop work in the UK?**
No single legal maximum for all work. Employers must assess risk and implement reasonable controls. Extreme cases may justify stopping via risk assessment.

**Does HSE inspect sites for heat in summer?**
Heat can appear in welfare checks during summer campaigns. Water, shade and rest are observable on walk-rounds.

**What trades are highest risk?**
Roofing, groundworks, steel fixing, plant operators in unventilated cabs, and anyone in heavy PPE on exposed sites.

**Should we buy a site thermometer?**
Useful for consistent triggers in your work-rest policy. WBGT meters are rare on UK building sites but workplace thermometers in the work area are practical.

**Do we need to change RAMS for heatwaves?**
If your method assumes winter PPE and single water point, update RAMS and brief when summer conditions apply.

**How does heat relate to RIDDOR?**
Heat stroke requiring hospital treatment may be reportable. Know your [RIDDOR duties](https://mysafeops.com/blog/riddor-changes-2026) and seek occupational health advice when unsure.
