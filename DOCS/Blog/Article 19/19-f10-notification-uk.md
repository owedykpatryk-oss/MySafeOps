It's Monday morning and the hoarding is up. The client has signed the contract, the principal contractor has mobilised, and the first delivery of plant is due Wednesday. Someone asks whether the job is notifiable under CDM 2015. The site manager opens the HSE website, finds the F10 form, and realises half the fields need information the client has not provided yet.

That scramble is normal on UK construction projects that cross the notification threshold. **F10** is the form you use to tell HSE that a notifiable project is about to start. It is not a permission slip: HSE does not approve your method. It is a legal record that the project meets CDM 2015 notification criteria and that key duty holders are named.

If you are a principal contractor, a small contractor wondering whether your client's job counts, or a domestic client who did not know notification existed, this guide covers when F10 is required, how to submit it, and what to keep on file.

## What F10 is and who submits it

Under CDM 2015, the **client** must notify HSE before construction work begins on a notifiable project. Regulation 6 sets the thresholds (see below). In practice:

- **Commercial clients** submit F10 themselves or through their project manager.
- **Domestic clients** pass CDM duties to the principal designer and principal contractor; notification still happens, usually coordinated by the PC.
- **Principal contractors** often complete the online form on the client's behalf: but the client's legal duty does not disappear. Get written confirmation the F10 was submitted.

F10 captures project address, planned start and end dates, names of the client, principal designer and principal contractor, and a brief description of the work. HSE uses it for planning inspections and linking projects to duty holders if something goes wrong later.

## When F10 is required

A construction project is **notifiable** if any one of these applies:

| Trigger | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| **Duration** | More than 30 working days |
| **Workforce** | More than 20 workers on site at any one time |
| **Person-days** | More than 500 person-days of construction work |

**Person-days** means one person working one day. Ten people for fifty days is 500 person-days. A six-month refurbishment with a crew of eight might cross the duration threshold even if daily headcount stays below twenty.

**Not notifiable:** short domestic extensions, small shop fit-outs under the thresholds, and maintenance jobs that do not meet any trigger. If you are unsure, err on the side of notifying: there is no penalty for notifying when you did not have to. Missing F10 on a notifiable project is a different story.

**When to submit:** before work starts. HSE expects notification in advance, not after the first concrete pour. On fast-track jobs, treat F10 as part of mobilisation alongside insurance, CPP issue and [site induction](https://mysafeops.com/blog/site-induction-software-uk).

## How to submit F10 to HSE online

HSE accepts F10 through its online notification system at [hse.gov.uk](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/cdm/notify.htm). The old paper F10 still exists in guidance, but online submission is standard.

**Step-by-step:**

1. **Confirm the project is notifiable** using the three triggers above.
2. **Gather duty holder details**: full names, addresses, and contact details for client, principal designer (if appointed) and principal contractor.
3. **Complete the online form** with project address, UPRN if known, description of work, and planned dates.
4. **Submit and save the confirmation**: screenshot or PDF the acknowledgement page with reference number.
5. **Distribute copies** to the principal designer, principal contractor and anyone keeping the project file (often the site office and client QS).

If the principal designer or principal contractor changes mid-project, you may need to notify HSE of the change. Check current HSE guidance: duty holder changes on notifiable projects are not something you leave until the next audit.

## What information you need before you start

Before you open the form, collect:

- **Client details**: legal entity name, registered address, contact. Domestic clients need the address where work happens.
- **Principal designer**: required on projects with more than one contractor unless the client justifies otherwise. Name, organisation, contact.
- **Principal contractor**: name, organisation, contact. On single-contractor jobs where the contractor is also the PC, that firm appears here.
- **Project description**: enough detail for HSE to understand scope: "demolition and rebuild of two-storey extension", not just "building work".
- **Dates**: realistic start and end. Update if the programme slips significantly; HSE guidance covers material changes.

Also have ready: the [Construction Phase Plan](https://mysafeops.com/blog/cdm-2015-small-contractor-uk) outline, pre-construction information pack status, and your approach to [permits to work](https://mysafeops.com/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk) if hot work, height or excavation are in the first weeks.

## Common mistakes on notifiable projects

- **Assuming the main contractor will "sort HSE"** without the client checking submission happened.
- **Counting calendar days instead of working days** for the 30-day trigger.
- **Forgetting person-days on phased work**: early trades plus later fit-out can cross 500 even if peak headcount is under 20.
- **No saved confirmation**: "we submitted it" with no reference number fails an audit.
- **F10 submitted after work started**: common on fast mobilisations; fix it the same day if you discover the gap.
- **Domestic client jobs**: notification still applies above thresholds; the PC usually drives it but someone must own it.

## F10 and the Construction Phase Plan

F10 and the CPP are separate legal requirements, but auditors treat them as a pair. The CPP describes how the PC will manage health and safety during construction. F10 tells HSE the project exists and who is in charge.

On a notifiable site, an HSE inspector may ask for:

- F10 confirmation
- Current Construction Phase Plan
- Pre-construction information
- Evidence of cooperation between PD and PC
- Site induction records and relevant permits

Digital systems help because the inspector does not wait while someone searches the cabin for a ring binder. Timestamped inductions, live [permit boards](https://mysafeops.com/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk) and a PDF of F10 on a tablet look organised. Disorganised sites get deeper inspections.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is responsible for submitting F10?**
The client under CDM 2015. Principal contractors often complete the form, but the client remains legally responsible for ensuring notification happens.

**How long before work starts should F10 be submitted?**
Before construction work begins. Treat it as a pre-start checklist item alongside insurance and site setup.

**What happens if we forgot to submit F10?**
Submit as soon as you realise. Continuing on a notifiable project without notification is a breach. HSE may take enforcement action, especially if other CDM failures appear on inspection.

**Is F10 the same as a Construction Phase Plan?**
No. F10 notifies HSE that a notifiable project is starting. The CPP is the principal contractor's plan for managing site health and safety.

**Do domestic projects need F10?**
Yes, if they meet the notifiable thresholds. CDM still applies; domestic client duties are usually transferred to the designer and contractor.

**Can I notify HSE by post?**
Online notification is the standard route. Check HSE's current guidance for alternatives if online access is genuinely impossible.
