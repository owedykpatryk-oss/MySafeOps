The demolition operative runs a breaker for three hours in a plant room. Nobody logged trigger time. The RAMS said "limit vibration exposure". By Friday his fingers go white in the cold. Ten years later the claim lands and the only evidence is a toolbox talk attendance sheet.

**Hand-arm vibration syndrome (HAVS)** is preventable industrial disease. On UK construction sites it is still treated as "part of the job". The Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005 apply on every project under general health and safety law, and clients increasingly ask for exposure evidence under **CDM 2015**.

This sits with [noise at work](/blog/noise-at-work-construction-uk), [silica dust](/blog/silica-dust-construction-uk), and [how to write a RAMS](/blog/how-to-write-a-rams-uk). Same exposure logic, different hazard.

## Why HAVS still gets missed

Vibration injury builds slowly. White finger, numbness, and loss of grip strength appear after repeated high exposure. By then the damage is permanent.

Common site failures:

- No tool inventory with manufacturer vibration magnitudes
- Exposure estimated from memory, not trigger time
- Gloves treated as the main control (they are not)
- Agency labour using the worst tools with no rotation
- Health surveillance delayed until someone complains

Principal contractors who coordinate multi-trade packages should treat HAVS like silica: named in the [Construction Phase Plan](/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk), not buried in a generic PPE appendix.

## Legal limits in plain English

HSE sets two key figures for daily hand-arm vibration exposure (A(8)):

| Measure | Value | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exposure Action Value (EAV) | 2.5 m/s² A(8) | Introduce a programme of controls and health surveillance |
| Exposure Limit Value (ELV) | 5 m/s² A(8) | Must not be exceeded |

Daily exposure depends on vibration magnitude of the tool and how long it is used. A high-vibration breaker can hit the action value in minutes, not hours.

Use manufacturer data or HSE ready-reckoners. Do not invent figures from "it did not feel that bad".

## High-risk tools and tasks

Typical UK site sources:

| Tool / task | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Breakers / demolition hammers | Very high magnitude, long trigger time |
| Angle grinders / cut-off saws | Continuous grip + vibration |
| Scabblers / needle guns | Concrete prep, high exposure |
| Impact wrenches / riveters | Short bursts that add up |
| Plate compactors (hand-guided) | Whole-body plus hand-arm risk |

If the method needs prolonged use of these tools, the [RAMS](/blog/how-to-write-a-rams-uk) must show exposure estimate, rotation, and alternative methods, not only "wear anti-vibration gloves".

## Hierarchy of control on site

Work down the list:

1. **Eliminate / reduce:** design out scabbling, use larger plant, cut off-site, hire lower-vibration tools
2. **Job design:** split breaking between operatives, schedule rest, warm hands (cold worsens symptoms)
3. **Tool selection:** maintain bits sharp; worn tooling increases vibration and time on the trigger
4. **Maintenance:** service schedule for breakers and grinders
5. **PPE last:** gloves help warmth and grip; they do not remove the duty to control exposure

## Health surveillance and records

When exposure is likely at or above the EAV, arrange suitable health surveillance (usually through occupational health). Keep:

- Tool list with vibration magnitudes
- Exposure assessments by task
- Training / briefing records
- Health surveillance referrals and outcomes (confidential medical detail held correctly)
- Evidence of tool rotation and maintenance

If someone reports tingling, numbness, or blanching, stop high-vibration work pending assessment. Do not wait for a RIDDOR classification debate — protect the worker first. See [RIDDOR changes 2026](/blog/riddor-changes-2026) for reporting context when disease is diagnosed.

## What HSE looks for

Expect questions on:

- How you estimated A(8) for the worst task this week
- Whether agency and subbie labour are in the same programme ([subcontractor coordination](/blog/subcontractor-management-software-uk))
- Maintenance of hired breakers
- Whether the CPP and site induction mention vibration controls
- Whether anyone is already under health surveillance

Paper folders that only say "minimise vibration" fail audits. Live records that show who used which tool for how long hold up better — same lesson as [digital vs paper RAMS](/blog/paper-vs-digital-rams-uk).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is HAVS?**
Hand-arm vibration syndrome: damage to nerves, blood vessels and joints from repeated hand-transmitted vibration. Symptoms include finger blanching, numbness and loss of dexterity.

**Do anti-vibration gloves fix the problem?**
No. They may help comfort. Legal duty is to reduce exposure at source and through job design. Gloves alone do not meet the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005.

**When is health surveillance required?**
When workers are likely to be exposed at or above the Exposure Action Value of 2.5 m/s² A(8), or when they report symptoms that may relate to vibration.

**How do I estimate daily exposure?**
Use manufacturer vibration data and measured or logged trigger time. HSE publishes ready-reckoners. Do not rely on "felt fine today".

**Should vibration be in the Construction Phase Plan?**
Yes where high-vibration work is planned. Name the controls, who monitors exposure, and how subbies are briefed.

**How does this link to noise and dust?**
Breakers often create vibration, noise and silica together. Control them as one package in the RAMS and permits, not three separate afterthoughts.
