# Pre-Construction Information UK: What Clients Must Provide Under CDM

*By the MySafeOps team · Last updated 8 July 2026 · 9 min read*

> **Key takeaways**
> - Pre-construction information (PCI) is the client's duty under CDM 2015 Regulation 4.
> - PCI feeds the principal designer's work and the principal contractor's Construction Phase Plan.
> - It should cover existing hazards on site: [asbestos](https://mysafeops.com/blog/asbestos-management-construction-uk), utilities, structural constraints, ground conditions.
> - Weak PCI causes mid-project surprises, utility strikes, and asbestos exposures.
> - PCs should request PCI in writing before mobilisation and record what was missing.

Mobilisation Monday. The excavation gang starts trial pits and hits a live cable not on any drawing the client sent. Work stops. The principal contractor opens the pre-start email chain and finds a one-page site address note labelled "PCI". No utility records. No asbestos survey. The client says the information was "available on request".

That failure sits with the **client** under CDM 2015, but the **principal contractor** owns the site when work starts. Gaps in **pre-construction information (PCI)** become your programme delay, your enforcement conversation, and your subcontractor claims.

This guide explains what UK clients must provide, what good PCI looks like, and how principal contractors fold it into the [Construction Phase Plan](https://mysafeops.com/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk).

## What pre-construction information is

**Pre-construction information** is everything the client already knows (or ought to know) about the project that designers and contractors need for planning and doing the work safely.

Regulation 4 requires the client to provide PCI **as soon as practicable** to every designer and contractor appointed, or being considered for appointment.

PCI is not:
- A full design pack (that comes from designers)
- A substitute for site-specific RAMS (contractors still write those)
- Optional on small jobs because "everyone knows the building"

PCI is the baseline hazard picture before anyone prices or sequences the work.

## Who provides PCI and when

| Duty holder | PCI role |
| --- | --- |
| **Client** | Gather and provide PCI; ensure it is updated |
| **Principal designer** | Help client identify what PCI is needed; use PCI in design risk management |
| **Principal contractor** | Receive PCI; reflect relevant content in CPP; brief subcontractors |
| **Designers / contractors** | Tell client if PCI is inadequate |

**Timing:** as early as possible, before detailed design and certainly before construction starts. On notifiable projects, link PCI receipt to [F10 notification](https://mysafeops.com/blog/f10-notification-uk) and CPP issue.

Domestic clients often do not know PCI exists. Duties may transfer; see [CDM 2015 for small contractors](https://mysafeops.com/blog/cdm-2015-small-contractor-uk).

## What PCI must contain

HSE guidance lists topics proportionate to the project. A useful PCI pack includes:

**1. Project description**
Scope, location, intended use, programme constraints.

**2. Existing structures and ground**
Layout drawings, load limits, known movement, contamination reports, ground investigation summary.

**3. Utilities and services**
Electricity, gas, water, telecoms, drainage. Surveys, plans, isolation points. Critical before [excavation permits](https://mysafeops.com/blog/excavation-permit-uk).

**4. Asbestos and hazardous materials**
Survey type (management/refurbishment/demolition), locations, condition. No survey where one is required is a red flag.

**5. Existing health and safety information**
Previous incidents, fragile roofs, confined spaces, fire compartmentation, occupied areas (hospitals, schools).

**6. Interface with adjacent sites or public**
Shared boundaries, highways, railways, neighbour agreements.

**7. Client constraints**
Access hours, noise limits, security, occupied building zones.

## PCI vs design information

| | Pre-construction information | Design information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source** | Client (existing site knowledge) | Designers (new work) |
| **Timing** | Early, pre-construction | Develops through design |
| **Content** | What is already there | What will be built and how |
| **Used by** | PD for design risk; PC for CPP | PD/contractors for construction method |

The **principal designer** produces health and safety information in the design; that complements PCI but does not replace missing client data about the existing building.

## Common gaps on UK projects

- **No utility search records** before civils price the job
- **Asbestos survey missing** on pre-2000 buildings
- **"TBC" structural drawings** at PC mobilisation
- **Occupied building risks** understated (night works in live hospital wing)
- **PDF dump without index**; nobody knows what is current
- **PCI never updated** after client change or new survey

During [HSE inspection season](https://mysafeops.com/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk), inspectors may ask how PCI informed the CPP and inductions.

## How PCs use PCI in the CPP

The [Construction Phase Plan](https://mysafeops.com/blog/construction-phase-plan-template-uk) should reference PCI explicitly:

- Section on significant site risks cites PCI sources ([asbestos locations](https://mysafeops.com/blog/asbestos-management-construction-uk), fragile roofs)
- Traffic and access reflects PCI constraints
- Emergency procedures note occupied building interfaces
- Subcontractor briefing lists which PCI sections each trade must read

Store PCI centrally in [CDM compliance software](https://mysafeops.com/blog/cdm-2015-compliance-software-uk) or a controlled folder. Version it when the client issues updates.

At induction, link PCI topics to live controls: where asbestos is flagged, how isolation is proved before [electrical work](https://mysafeops.com/blog/electrical-isolation-permit-uk).

## Pushing back on incomplete PCI

Practical steps for principal contractors:

1. **PCI request list in tender/appointment stage** with standard HSE topic checklist
2. **Written hold on mobilisation** until minimum utilities and asbestos status confirmed
3. **Gap register** listing missing items, sent to client and PD weekly
4. **Do not assume liability** for client information gaps in subcontract terms without legal advice
5. **Update CPP** when PCI arrives late; re-brief affected trades

If PCI is weak, document the pushback. Auditors and HSE ask whether the PC acted on what they knew, and whether they flagged what they did not.

For PC duty context see [principal contractor duties UK](https://mysafeops.com/blog/principal-contractor-duties-uk).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is responsible for pre-construction information?**
The client under CDM 2015 Regulation 4. Designers and contractors should prompt the client if information is missing.

**When should PCI be provided?**
As soon as practicable, before design and construction planning. Before work starts on site.

**Is PCI required on small domestic jobs?**
CDM applies proportionately. Even small projects need relevant existing hazard information, though the pack may be short.

**What if the client says they have no information?**
Document the gap. Commission surveys if contract allows. Do not start high-risk work (demolition, excavation) blind.

**How does PCI relate to the Construction Phase Plan?**
PCI informs the CPP. The CPP explains how site-wide arrangements manage risks identified in PCI and design.

**Should subcontractors receive the full PCI pack?**
They should receive the sections relevant to their work, briefed at induction with access to full pack on request.
