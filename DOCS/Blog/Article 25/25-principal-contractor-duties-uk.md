# Principal Contractor Duties UK: CDM 2015 Regulations 12-14 Explained

*By the MySafeOps team · Last updated 8 July 2026 · 10 min read*

> **Key takeaways**
> - The principal contractor runs the construction phase when more than one contractor is on site (or when appointed on notifiable projects).
> - Regulation 13 lists management duties: CPP, induction, welfare, coordination, RAMS checks, designer liaison.
> - Regulation 14 covers workers: information, training, cooperation, and preventing unauthorised access.
> - Most prosecutions trace back to planning and coordination gaps, not exotic hazards.
> - Evidence matters: inductions, permits, CPP updates, and cooperation records.

Friday afternoon on a mixed-use block. The scaffolder wants an early finish, the M&E subbie is pushing for ceiling access, and the client's H&S adviser emails asking for proof that last week's design change was passed to trades. The site manager is the de facto **principal contractor**. Everyone assumes CDM is "the H&S folder". In practice it is who coordinates what happens on site when more than one firm shares the same air.

**CDM 2015** splits duties between client, principal designer and principal contractor. Regulations 12 to 14 define the PC role. This guide translates those regulations into what a UK principal contractor actually does each week, and how to evidence it without drowning in paperwork.

## Who is the principal contractor

The **principal contractor** is appointed by the client to plan, manage and coordinate the construction phase when:

- There is more than one contractor on the project, or
- The project is notifiable and the client appoints a PC (standard on commercial work)

On many small jobs, the main contractor becomes PC by default when the domestic client does not appoint anyone. See [CDM 2015 for small contractors](https://mysafeops.com/blog/cdm-2015-small-contractor-uk) for that angle.

The PC does not do every trade's RAMS. They coordinate site-wide arrangements so subcontractors can work without tripping over each other's hazards.

| Role | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Client** | Provides PCI, appoints PD/PC, allows time and resource |
| **Principal designer** | Pre-construction design risk management |
| **Principal contractor** | Construction phase planning, coordination, site rules |
| **Subcontractors** | Their own RAMS, competence, supervision |

## Regulation 12: client appointment

Regulation 12 is technically a **client duty**: appoint a principal contractor before construction begins where there is more than one contractor, and on notifiable projects.

**Why PCs should care:** if you mobilised without a written PC appointment, clarify it day one. Ambiguity surfaces in contract disputes and HSE interviews.

Checklist for PC appointment:

- Written appointment in contract or pre-start minutes
- Scope matches what you control on site
- [F10 notification](https://mysafeops.com/blog/f10-notification-uk) completed if notifiable
- [Pre-construction information](https://mysafeops.com/blog/pre-construction-information-uk) received from client

## Regulation 13: management of the construction phase

Regulation 13 is the core PC duty list. In plain English:

**1. Prepare, review and update the Construction Phase Plan**
Before work starts on notifiable projects; maintain it as the site evolves. Link to live [permits](https://mysafeops.com/blog/permit-to-work-app-uk) and significant risks.

**2. Organise cooperation between contractors**
Site meetings, interface agreements, shared areas (crane lifts, hoists, corridors). Document decisions that affect multiple trades.

**3. Coordinate work so health and safety is managed**
Sequencing, laydown, temporary works interfaces, hot work near combustibles, excavation near footings. This is where [HSE inspections](https://mysafeops.com/blog/hse-inspection-construction-uk) look for evidence of active management.

**4. Apply site rules and carry out site induction**
Every worker and visitor who needs it. [Site induction software](https://mysafeops.com/blog/site-induction-software-uk) or paper, but records must be retrievable.

**5. Provide welfare and prevent unauthorised access**
Toilets, water, rest, fencing, signing in. Summer welfare includes heat; see [summer heat safety](https://mysafeops.com/blog/summer-heat-safety-construction-uk).

**6. Give subcontractors relevant parts of the CPP and PCI**
Not a generic email blast. Trades need the sections that affect their work.

**7. Ensure subcontractors have adequate RAMS**
Review and challenge weak RAMS before work starts. File approvals.

**8. Liaise with the principal designer**
Share site feedback on residual design risks; update when design changes mid-project.

**9. Display the F10 notification certificate**
On notifiable projects, where HSE guidance expects it visible.

## Regulation 14: duties to workers

Regulation 14 applies to contractors generally; the PC must ensure it happens across the site:

- Workers receive suitable **information, instruction and training**
- Workers **cooperate** with site rules and each other
- **Unauthorised access** is prevented
- **Welfare** is provided (overlaps Reg 13)

For PCs this means induction content matches real hazards, toolbox talks happen when conditions change ([digital toolbox talks](https://mysafeops.com/blog/digital-toolbox-talks)), and agency staff get the same briefing as direct employees.

## Daily PC tasks that satisfy CDM

Regulations read like a textbook. A typical week on site looks like:

| Day | PC / site manager actions |
| --- | --- |
| **Monday** | Review programme, update CPP if layout changed, check permits for the week |
| **Daily** | Gate briefing for new arrivals, walk high-risk areas, close or extend permits |
| **Mid-week** | Subbie coordination meeting, chase RAMS gaps, log design queries to PD |
| **Friday** | Review open permits, induction register vs attendance, plan next week welfare |

None of this requires a law degree. It requires a named person with authority and records that survive an audit.

## Common gaps HSE and clients find

- **PC appointed late or informally** while multiple subbies already on site
- **CPP generic** and not updated after programme shift
- **PCI never received** from client; asbestos or utilities surprise mid-project
- **Induction gaps** for agency labour on day three
- **RAMS approved in email** but never briefed on site
- **No designer liaison** when structural or M&E changes land
- **Permits disconnected from coordination** (crane lift over hot work area)

Enforcement often stacks CDM Reg 13 failures with specific regulations (WAHR, COSHH, etc.). Fix coordination and many secondary findings shrink.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a principal contractor under CDM 2015?**
The contractor appointed to plan, manage and coordinate the construction phase when there is more than one contractor, or on notifiable projects as appointed by the client.

**What is the difference between principal contractor and principal designer?**
The principal designer manages pre-construction design risk. The principal contractor manages the construction phase on site. Both cooperate through the project.

**Can a subcontractor be principal contractor?**
Yes, if the client appoints them and they have the organisational capability to coordinate the whole construction phase.

**What documents must a principal contractor maintain?**
At minimum: Construction Phase Plan, induction records, subcontractor RAMS approvals, cooperation records, F10 confirmation if notifiable, and links to permits and briefings for high-risk work.

**What happens if there is no appointed principal contractor?**
Duties may fall to the client or contractor in control. Gaps in coordination are a common enforcement theme when multiple trades work without a named PC.

**How do principal contractor duties differ on domestic projects?**
Duties still apply above thresholds. Domestic clients often transfer appointments to the contractor; the PC role may sit with the main builder by default.
